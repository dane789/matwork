THE LOOP WE’RE BUILDING

Learn it.
Work on it.
Bring it back.

The planned Matwork loop gives each person a clear way to contribute and gives the coach better context for the session after that.

  1. 1
    Coach captures the lesson

    Quick notes preserve the actual cues, priorities, and assignments. AI can help organize a draft for the coach to review and approve.

  2. 2
    Wrestler gets a focused plan

    Clear practice items and lesson takeaways encourage ownership, without burying the athlete in parent-facing detail.

  3. 3
    Parent shares their lens

    Parents can keep notes private or intentionally share observations and expectations. Shared notes stay labeled as the parent’s perspective.

  4. 4
    Coach plans the next session

    The coach receives the wrestler’s reflection, completed work, and selected family input—then translates what fits into coach-led cues and the next plan.

A BETTER COACHING EXPERIENCE

Keep the relationship active.
Keep coaching the focus.

Private coaches are focused on the wrestler and the work—not paperwork after every session. Matwork is designed to make a professional lesson experience easier to deliver: capture what you worked on, review an organized summary, and give the family a clear way to reinforce it.

As the shared feedback loop is built, coaches will be able to see completed work and the observations the wrestler and parent choose to share. That context can keep the conversation moving, help the next private feel purposeful, and make ongoing coaching easier for families to value. The goal is less administration around each session and more time for coaches to teach, train, and build the wrestler.
